# Name the Witness

Every name, comment, type, error, and event is a claim about state. Six moments, in the order the work meets them.

## Word or thing: is this layer a claim?

Alfred Korzybski, A Non-Aristotelian System (1931), quoted in [Map–territory relation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Map%E2%80%93territory_relation):

> the map is not the territory

> the word is not the thing

> A map *is not* the territory it represents, but, if correct, it has a *similar structure* to the territory, which accounts for its usefulness.

Sort each layer you write: fact or claim. A name, a comment, a message is a map of the code, not the code.

## Parse: does the claim carry its proof?

King's rule for carrying proof in the type, [Parse, don't validate](https://lexi-lambda.github.io/blog/2019/11/05/parse-don-t-validate/):

> you can think of a value of type `NonEmpty a` as being like a value of type `[a]`, plus a *proof* that the list is non-empty.

> Both of these functions check the same thing, but `parseNonEmpty` gives the caller access to the information it learned, while `validateNonEmpty` just throws it away.

the [Curry–Howard correspondence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry%E2%80%93Howard_correspondence), proofs as programs:

> a proof is a program, and the formula it proves is the type for the program.

Produce claims that carry their witness: check at the boundary and let the resulting type testify what passed. A verdict (`validate`, `sanitize`, `normalize`) discards what was learned; a parse keeps it.

## Contract: what can convict this claim?

Meyer's [design by contract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_by_contract):

> formal, precise and verifiable interface specifications for software components, which extend the ordinary definition of abstract data types with preconditions, postconditions and invariants.

> the "three questions" that the designer must repeatedly answer in the contract:
> * What does the contract expect?
> * What does the contract guarantee?
> * What does the contract maintain?

Where construction cannot carry the witness, attach a claim the runtime can convict: an assertion, a precondition, an invariant, an enumerable error tag.

## Log or view: does the record hold facts?

Fowler on rebuilding state from the log, [Event Sourcing](https://martinfowler.com/eaaDev/EventSourcing.html):

> Event Sourcing ensures that all changes to application state are stored as a sequence of events.

> Since an application state is purely derivable from the event log, you can cache it anywhere you like.

> The official system of record can either be the event logs or the current application state.

Store facts; derive interpretations. An unwitnessed claim is a view: emit it at the boundary, label it, keep it rebuildable. Never write a view into the record channel.

## Single representation: how many places say this?

Hunt and Thomas's [DRY principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don%27t_repeat_yourself):

> Every piece of knowledge must have a single, unambiguous, authoritative representation within a system.

[Hyrum's Law](https://www.hyrumslaw.com/):

> With a sufficient number of users of an API, it does not matter what you promise in the contract: all observable behaviors of your system will be depended on by somebody.

Keep one authoritative form. A docstring restating code is a second source that drifts, and readers depend on written claims whether or not anything checks them.

## Narrated: who wrote this claim, and what did they see?

Farnam Street on Taleb's [narrative fallacy](https://fs.blog/narrative-fallacy/):

> These types of stories strike a deep chord: They give us deep, affecting reasons on which to hang our understanding of reality. They help us make sense of our own lives. And, most importantly, they frequently cause us to believe we can predict the future. The problem is, most of them are a sham.

Wikipedia, [Hallucination (artificial intelligence)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucination_(artificial_intelligence)):

> a response generated by AI that contains false or misleading information presented as fact.

Ask who narrated the claim and what they observed. Generated prose is narration produced without observation: it enters the channel dressed as fact with no witness behind it.
